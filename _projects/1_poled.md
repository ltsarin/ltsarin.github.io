Germline and somatic mutations in *POLE* and *POLD1* compromise the proofreading activity of the
replicative DNA polymerases and give rise to tumors with an extremely high mutational burden.
Those tumors often respond well to immunotherapy — which makes calling a patient's *POLE*/*POLD1*
status correctly a decision with real clinical consequences.

The problem is that the evidence needed to make that call is scattered. Over two decades, variants
have been characterized in biochemical assays, in yeast, in mammalian cells, and in mouse tumor
models, across papers that are difficult for a non-specialist to weigh against one another.

**PolED** is my attempt to fix that. Through manual curation of the primary literature, it collects
what is actually known about the function of each clinically relevant *POLE* and *POLD1* variant —
the assays performed, the phenotypes observed, the supporting references — and pairs it with a
concise summary of functional significance.

- **Web resource:** [poled-db.org](https://poled-db.org)
- **Paper:** [Database (Oxford), 2025](https://doi.org/10.1093/database/baaf076)
