Man's Search for Meaning

Viktor E. Frankl  ·  1946

2026-06  ·  2026-07  · 

2026-06  ·  philosophy   psychology  ·  non-fiction


The part I keep coming back to

One particular moment is seared into my memory. The majority of concentration camp survivors, who dreamed daily about getting liberated, had an even harder time coping with life once outside the camp. “Why?” you might ask. Among all the possible reasons, the one I want to highlight is this: to survive, people hold onto a future goal (meaning) and what happens if that goal turns out to be gone? What if the play isn’t worth the candle? A prisoner pictures his wife every day and endures anything hoping he will see her again, but eventually learns she has perished in another camp. One may expect that after liberation, they will return to the life they used to live, but that life turns out to be far different from what they expected.

Why? A personal note

I could relate to these feelings deeply, as this is exactly what I went through when I was finally liberated after being trapped in Ukraine for years due to the War (males over 18, being subject to martial law, couldn’t leave the country and were sent to the battlefield once they turned 25). You may think nothing could be worse than having your future predefined, but the wrong expectations of “life outside” that kept you going for years can indeed be. For some, the day of liberation is an endpoint; for the majority, though, it is the beginning of the search for new meanings.
